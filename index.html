Rule: Kalshi only, no confirming book. Side priced at or above 0.20 mid, with Kalshi mid-CLV vs Pinnacle fair in [0.05, 0.12). One bet per fixture, first qualifying pre-kickoff snapshot, settled at the real ask — same non-look-ahead selection as xlow, so the two are comparable bet-for-bet.
Why it exists: xlow is unmeasurable, not merely unproven — +0.8% ROI against a 1.94u per-bet stdev needs ~100,000 bets. The binding constraint is variance, and unit-bet variance scales as (1−p)/p, so excluding long shots is what makes a rule testable at all: ~201,000 bets required without the floor, ~480 with it.
Provenance: xhigh was CHOSEN by looking at the post-2026-07-05 slice the xlow test runs on, so its selection numbers (+15.1%, z+1.33) are discovery, not validation — that data is in-sample for this rule and never counts. Only fixtures after 2026-08-09 are evidence. 17 candidates were swept and none reached z>1.96; what earned the freeze is that the price floor helped every rule family at every persistence level, and that it makes the question answerable in weeks. xhigh also overlaps xlow — both fire on Kalshi CLV in the same band — so these are not independent bets.

Read the Spread column, not the P&L. A complete book collects the overround whoever wins, so it lands in a narrow band; a partial book is a naked position on one outcome and swings ~25× wider for a mean that is not distinguishable from zero. That is why completion rate is the headline and total P&L is not: the profit is a near-certainty and all the variance is partial fills. Modelled per-book above is completion×complete + (1−completion)×partial — both measured far more precisely than realised P&L, which would need ~841 books at the current gate to resolve.

Each row asks: if we had only quoted books whose thinnest leg had at least this much traded volume, what would have happened? Completion improves monotonically up to roughly 500–1,000 and then reverses — the highest threshold has the best completion rate and still loses money, plausibly adverse selection, since the busiest markets are where an informed counterparty is most likely to be the one lifting us. Changes which books are quoted, not how they are priced, so it is a regime not a tweak: on 2026-08-09 it admitted 4 of 30 otherwise-playable books. Aimed at partial fills, which is where the losses live — a 2-of-3 book runs −7.62c while a complete one runs +1.38c.
Regimes are different strategies, not tweaks — pooling them once produced a “today’s maker result” that blended three configurations logged hours apart. Each book carries the regime stamped at post time, so a mixed day cannot silently average them. Compare within a regime, never across.
A complete book earns the overround and is outcome-independent; a stranded one is a naked position on whichever side the market is moving toward — which is why 2-of-3 is the worst state, not 1-of-3. The gate quotes only books liquid enough to finish: on this history, min leg volume ≥500 lifted completion 36% → 60% and turned −1.84c/book into +3.01c. That threshold was chosen on this same data, so it is in-sample — it is frozen from 2026-08-09 to be judged on fresh books only.
